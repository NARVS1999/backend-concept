Part One: Childhood in Maycomb — Lesson 2 of 8

Chapter 2 — First Day of School

Scout's disastrous introduction to formal education

Focus Scout's first day of school is a disaster. Miss Caroline Fisher, the new teacher from Winston County, doesn't understand Maycomb's ways. She scolds Scout for already knowing how to read, and the Cunningham family's pride becomes a lesson in poverty and dignity.

Key Details

  • Miss Caroline: young, inexperienced, from Northern Alabama — doesn't fit in
  • Scout can read and write — Atticus taught her by reading together each night
  • Miss Caroline: "Your father does not know how to teach"
  • Walter Cunningham refuses lunch money — Cunningham family pride
  • Scout gets punished for explaining the Cunninghams to Miss Caroline
  • Theme: formal education vs. the education of home and experience
  • Scout is smarter than the teacher but gets in trouble for it

Study Task

Analysis Exercise

Contrast the education Scout receives at home (from Atticus) with what she experiences at school. What is Lee suggesting about different kinds of intelligence?

Important Quote

"I never went to school but once" — Scout (ironic, since she was eager to go)

Scout's disappointment with school contrasts with her rich education at home.

Summary
