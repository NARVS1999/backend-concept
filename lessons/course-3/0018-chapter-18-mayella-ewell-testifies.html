Part Three: The Trial — Lesson 7 of 10

Chapter 18 — Mayella Ewell Testifies

The accuser's story falls apart

Focus Mayella takes the stand — lonely, scared, and lying. Atticus treats her with courtesy but exposes the cracks in her story.

Key Details

  • Mayella is 19: lonely, responsible for many siblings
  • She accuses Tom of beating and raping her
  • She asked Tom inside to "fix a door that didn't need fixing"
  • Mayella admits there was "nothing wrong with the door"
  • She refuses to answer what happened with her siblings
  • Tom's crippled left arm: can't use it, can't have caused the injuries
  • Mayella is both liar and victim: she's afraid of her father

Study Task

Analysis Exercise

Mayella is both the accuser and a victim. How does Lee create sympathy for her while still maintaining Tom's innocence?

Important Quote

"She was as sad as what Atticus said" — Scout on Mayella

Even Scout recognizes Mayella's tragedy — she is a victim of her father's abuse and her own lies.

Summary
