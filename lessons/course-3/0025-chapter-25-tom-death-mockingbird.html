Part Four: Aftermath and Resolution — Lesson 4 of 10

Chapter 25 — Tom's Death and the Mockingbird

The mockingbird metaphor returns

Focus Mr. Underwood writes an editorial comparing Tom's death to the "senseless slaughter of songbirds." The mockingbird theme is fully articulated.

Key Details

  • Mr. Underwood's editorial: compares Tom's death to killing a mockingbird
  • "He was trying to protect himself" — Tom ran, was shot
  • The town: "Typical of a nigger to cut and run"
  • "In the secret courts of men's hearts Atticus had no case"
  • The mockingbird: Tom is innocent, killed for no reason

Study Task

Analysis Exercise

Why is Tom a "mockingbird"? How does Mr. Underwood's editorial connect to the title of the novel?

Important Quote

"In the secret courts of men's hearts Atticus had no case." — Scout's narration

The law may have failed, but Atticus was also defeated in the "secret courts" of human prejudice — a deeper tragedy.

Summary
