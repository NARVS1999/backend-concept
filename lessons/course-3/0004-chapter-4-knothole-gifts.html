Part One: Childhood in Maycomb — Lesson 4 of 8

Chapter 4 — The Knothole Gifts

The Radley oak yields its first secrets

Focus Scout discovers gum and polished pennies in the Radley oak's knothole — someone is leaving gifts. The children invent the "Boo Radley" game, acting out the Radley family history.

Key Details

  • First knothole gift: Wrigley's Double-Mint gum (Scout is suspicious)
  • Second gift: two polished Indian-head pennies
  • The knothole is in the Radley oak tree on the Radley property line
  • The "Boo Radley" game: acting out Radley family stories
  • Scout feels the game is wrong but can't articulate why
  • Atticus catches them playing but doesn't stop them directly
  • The gifts connect Boo to the children — a silent communication

Study Task

Analysis Exercise

What do the knothole gifts suggest about Boo Radley's personality and intentions? Why does Scout feel uneasy about the Boo Radley game?

Important Quote

"I sometimes think of the Radley tree" — Scout, retrospectively

Looking back, Scout recognizes the significance of the tree as a symbol of Boo's connection to them.

Summary

  • The children's "Boo Radley game" reduces a real person to folklore
