Part Four: Aftermath and Resolution — Lesson 9 of 10

Chapter 30 — Standing in His Shoes

Protecting the mockingbird

Focus Heck Tate insists Bob Ewell fell on his own knife — protecting Boo from the public eye. Scout walks Boo home and stands on his porch, seeing the world from his perspective.

Key Details

  • Heck Tate: Bob Ewell "fell on his knife" — case closed
  • Atticus thinks Jem killed Ewell — wants to protect Jem
  • Tate: "I never heard tell it was against the law for a man to try and save his own life"
  • Tate insists Ewell's death stay quiet — to protect Boo
  • "Let the dead bury the dead"
  • Exposing Boo would be "like shootin' a mockingbird"
  • Scout on the Radley porch: she sees the street from Boo's view

Study Task

Analysis Exercise

Why does Tate insist on protecting Boo? Connect this to the mockingbird theme — who is the mockingbird in this chapter?

Important Quote

"Let the dead bury the dead" — Heck Tate

Tate argues that exposing Boo to public scrutiny would destroy him — it's better to leave the tragedy buried.
