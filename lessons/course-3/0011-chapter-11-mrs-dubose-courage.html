Part Two: The Mockingbird Theme — Lesson 3 of 3

Chapter 11 — Mrs. Dubose and Real Courage

Atticus redefines what it means to be brave

Focus Mrs. Dubose insults Atticus for defending Tom. Jem destroys her camellia bushes and is sentenced to read to her daily. She dies, and Atticus reveals she was fighting morphine addiction — the bravest person he ever knew.

Key Details

  • Mrs. Dubose: cantankerous, racist, old and ill
  • Calls Atticus a "nigger-lover" — Jem destroys her camellia bushes
  • Punishment: read to Mrs. Dubose every day for a month
  • Mrs. Dubose is dying and addicted to morphine
  • She wanted to "die beholden to nothing and nobody"
  • "Real courage is when you know you're licked before you begin but you begin anyway and see it through no matter what"
  • She leaves Jem a camellia — forgiveness and recognition

Study Task

Analysis Exercise

Compare Mrs. Dubose's courage with Atticus's. What is Atticus's definition of "real courage"?

Important Quote

"I wanted you to see what real courage is, instead of getting the idea that courage is a man with a gun in his hand." — Atticus

Atticus redefines courage as moral endurance — fighting a battle you know you'll lose because it's right.

Quiz

Summary

  • Mrs. Dubose's battle with addiction teaches Jem about real courage
  • Atticus redefines bravery as moral strength, not physical power
  • The camellia symbolizes forgiveness and recognition of true victory
