Part Three: The Trial — Lesson 5 of 10

Chapter 16 — The Trial Begins

Maycomb gathers for the spectacle of justice

Focus The town gathers for the trial. Scout, Jem, and Dill sneak into the courtroom and watch from the Black balcony.

Key Details

  • "It was a gala occasion" — the trial as entertainment and spectacle
  • People from all over the county flood Maycomb
  • Courtroom is segregated: Black gallery upstairs
  • Reverend Sykes saves seats for the children in the colored balcony
  • Judge Taylor: old, sleepy-looking, misses nothing
  • Dolphus Raymond: white man who prefers Black company

Study Task

Analysis Exercise

Why does the town treat the trial as a public spectacle? How does the segregated courtroom reflect Maycomb's social structure?

Important Quote

"It was a gala occasion" — Scout's narration

The irony is sharp — a man's life is on trial, but the white community treats it as entertainment.

Summary
