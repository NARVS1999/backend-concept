Part Three: The Trial — Lesson 2 of 10

Chapter 13 — Aunt Alexandra Arrives

Tradition and "gentle breeding" come to the Finch house

Focus Aunt Alexandra moves in to provide "feminine influence" for Scout. She brings Finch family pride, Southern tradition, and tension with Atticus.

Key Details

  • Alexandra arrives to help raise the children
  • Obsessed with "gentle breeding" — family heritage, social status
  • The Finches are "ancient and rich" — actually just old, not wealthy
  • She wants Atticus to fire Calpurnia — Atticus refuses
  • "Alexandra, Calpurnia's not leaving this house until she wants to"
  • Jem: "I think I'm beginning to understand why Boo Radley stays shut up... it's because he wants to stay inside"

Study Task

Analysis Exercise

Jem says he understands why Boo stays inside — "he wants to." What does Jem mean? How does Alexandra's arrival change the Finch house?

Important Quote

"I think I'm beginning to understand why Boo Radley stays shut up... it's because he wants to stay inside." — Jem

Jem recognizes that sometimes people isolate themselves to escape the pressures and hypocrisies of society.

Summary

  • Aunt Alexandra represents traditional Southern womanhood and class consciousness
  • She creates tension in the Finch household
  • Jem's empathy for Boo deepens
