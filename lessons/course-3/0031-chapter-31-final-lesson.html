Chapter 31 — The Final Lesson

Scout's moral education is complete

Focus Scout reflects on the novel's lessons as she returns home. Atticus reads to her, and the story ends with Scout safe, Jem healing.

Key Details

  • Scout walks Boo home, stands on his porch
  • "I had never seen our neighborhood from this angle"
  • She reflects: Boo has been watching them all these years
  • "One time he said you never really know a man until you stand in his shoes and walk around in them"
  • Atticus reads The Grey Ghost to Scout
  • Atticus: "Most people are nice, Scout, when you finally see them"
  • "He turned out the light and went into Jem's room. He would be there all night, and he would be there when Jem waked up in the morning."

Study Task

Analysis Exercise

Scout stands on the Radley porch and sees the world from Boo's perspective. What is the final lesson she learns? How has she grown from Chapter 1 to Chapter 31?

Important Quote

"Most people are nice, Scout, when you finally see them." — Atticus

This is the novel's final moral — not naive optimism, but a hard-won belief that empathy reveals the humanity in everyone.
