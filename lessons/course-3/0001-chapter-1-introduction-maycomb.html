Part One: Childhood in Maycomb — Lesson 1 of 8

Chapter 1 — Introduction to Maycomb

Setting the stage — the Radley Place, the Finch family, and the arrival of Dill

Focus First-person narrator Scout Finch introduces Maycomb, Alabama during the Depression. She lives with her father Atticus (a lawyer) and older brother Jem. They meet Dill, a summer visitor obsessed with the Radley Place — a haunted house occupied by the mysterious Boo Radley, a recluse who hasn't been seen in years.

Key Details

  • Narrator: Jean Louise "Scout" Finch (age ~6), told retrospectively by adult Scout
  • Setting: Maycomb, Alabama, 1930s — tired, old, poor Southern town
  • Atticus: widower, respected lawyer, unusually old parent (~50)
  • Jem: 10 years old, protective older brother
  • Calpurnia: Finch cook and housekeeper, strict but loving
  • Dill (Charles Baker Harris): based on Harper Lee's childhood friend Truman Capote
  • Boo Radley (Arthur Radley): local legend — stabbed his father with scissors, hasn't been seen in years
  • Gothic elements: the closed shutters, the folklore, the mystery

Study Task

Analysis Exercise

What specific details make the Radley Place frightening to the children? Identify three Gothic elements in this chapter that establish the mood of the novel.

Important Quote

"People said he existed, but Jem and I had never seen him." — Scout on Boo Radley

This line establishes Boo as a legend rather than a real person — the children know him only through rumor and fear.
