Part One: Childhood in Maycomb — Lesson 5 of 8

Chapter 5 — Miss Maudie and the Note

A more truthful perspective on Boo Radley

Focus Miss Maudie Atkinson provides a more humane perspective on Boo Radley. The children attempt to deliver a note to Boo through his window but are caught by Atticus.

Key Details

  • Miss Maudie: neighbor, friend to children, shares Atticus's moral clarity
  • "Mockingbirds don't do one thing but make music for us to enjoy" — early hint of the title
  • Maudie knew Boo as a boy: "I remember Arthur Radley when he was a boy"
  • Boo's father was a "foot-washing Baptist" — extremely religious, kept Boo inside
  • Boo's teenage "crime": associating with the wrong crowd, a minor legal incident
  • Children try to deliver a note via fishing pole — Atticus catches them
  • Atticus: "Stop tormenting that man"

Study Task

Analysis Exercise

Compare Miss Maudie's version of Boo Radley with the children's monster version. Why does Atticus stop them from delivering the note?

Important Quote

"Mockingbirds don't do one thing but make music for us to enjoy." — Miss Maudie

This is the first mention of the mockingbird — a symbol of innocence and harmlessness that becomes the novel's central metaphor.

Summary

  • Miss Maudie offers a more compassionate view of Boo as a misunderstood boy
  • The mockingbird symbol is introduced
  • Atticus teaches boundaries — Boo deserves privacy and respect
