Part Two: The Mockingbird Theme — Lesson 1 of 3

Chapter 9 — Atticus Takes the Case

Atticus agrees to defend Tom Robinson

Focus Atticus agrees to defend Tom Robinson, a Black man accused of raping a white woman. Scout faces taunts at school and at the family Christmas gathering.

Key Details

  • Scout fights Cecil Jacobs for saying Atticus "defends niggers"
  • Atticus: "I'm simply defending a Negro"
  • "I couldn't hold my head up in town" — Atticus explains his conscience
  • Christmas at Finch's Landing: Aunt Alexandra, Uncle Jack, Francis
  • Francis calls Atticus a "nigger-lover" — Scout punches him
  • Atticus: "When a child asks you something, answer him"
  • Atticus's legal strategy: the Ewells are lying

Study Task

Analysis Exercise

Atticus says he couldn't "hold up his head" if he didn't defend Tom. Is he guided more by his conscience, his reputation, or his sense of duty? What's the difference?

Important Quote

"I couldn't hold my head up in town" — Atticus explaining why he must defend Tom

Atticus links his self-respect to doing what is right, even when it is unpopular.
