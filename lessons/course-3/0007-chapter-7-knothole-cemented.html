Part One: Childhood in Maycomb — Lesson 7 of 8

Chapter 7 — The Knothole Cemented

Boo's connection is cut off

Focus The knothole gifts continue — soap carvings of Scout and Jem, a pocket watch, twine. But Nathan Radley cements the knothole, cutting off Boo's connection to the children.

Key Details

  • Jem finally reveals: his pants were mended and hung neatly on the fence
  • More gifts: gray twine, soap carvings (two figures that look like Scout and Jem)
  • More gum, a medal, an old pocket watch with an aluminum knife
  • The soap carvings are unmistakably them — Boo has been watching and caring
  • Jem writes a thank-you note → finds the knothole filled with cement
  • Nathan Radley: "Tree's dying. You plug 'em with cement when they're sick"
  • Atticus notes the tree looks healthy — Jem understands Nathan lied

Study Task

Analysis Exercise

Nathan Radley cements the knothole to prevent Boo from communicating with the children. What does this act reveal about Nathan? Why does Jem cry?

Important Quote

"He wanted to stay connected" — Jem's understanding of Boo's intent

Jem, more than Scout, understands that the gifts were Boo's way of reaching out — and Nathan's cementing is a cruel rejection.
