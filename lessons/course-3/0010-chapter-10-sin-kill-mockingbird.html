Part Two: The Mockingbird Theme — Lesson 2 of 3

Chapter 10 — It's a Sin to Kill a Mockingbird

The title is explained

Focus Jem and Scout think Atticus is old and useless — until he shoots a rabid dog with one shot, revealing he was once "One-Shot Finch." The title phrase is introduced.

Key Details

  • Jem and Scout are embarrassed by Atticus — he's old and doesn't do anything
  • Tim Johnson (rabid dog) staggers down the street
  • Heck Tate gives the rifle to Atticus — won't shoot it himself
  • Atticus: one shot, instant kill
  • Miss Maudie: "Your father's the deadest shot in Maycomb County"
  • He stopped hunting because he had an unfair advantage
  • "It's a sin to kill a mockingbird" (Miss Maudie explains)

Study Task

Analysis Exercise

Connect the title to this chapter. What makes a mockingbird innocent? Who are the "mockingbirds" in the novel so far — characters who harm no one?

Important Quote

"It's a sin to kill a mockingbird." — Miss Maudie

This explains the title — mockingbirds are innocent creatures that do no harm, making their destruction a moral sin.

Summary

  • Atticus reveals his hidden talent — the best shot in Maycomb
  • The mockingbird metaphor is introduced
  • "It's a sin to kill a mockingbird" becomes the novel's central moral
