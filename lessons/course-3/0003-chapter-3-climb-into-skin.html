Part One: Childhood in Maycomb — Lesson 3 of 8

Chapter 3 — Climb Into His Skin

Atticus's most important lesson — empathy

Focus Scout catches Walter Cunningham for lunch at the Finch house, Calpurnia teaches Scout a lesson in hospitality, and the Ewell family is introduced. Atticus gives Scout the most important advice in the novel: empathy.

Key Details

  • Scout beats up Walter for getting her in trouble — Jem invites him to dinner
  • Calpurnia's lesson: "Don't matter who they are, anybody sets foot in this house's yo' comp'ny"
  • Atticus's compromise: keep reading with him, but keep going to school
  • Burris Ewell: comes to school once a year, infested with cooties
  • Bob Ewell: town drunk, doesn't work, children neglected — society tolerates it
  • "You never really understand a person until you consider things from his point of view... until you climb into his skin and walk around in it"

Study Task

Analysis Exercise

Atticus's "climb into his skin" advice is the novel's moral center. How does Scout apply (or fail to apply) this principle in this chapter?

Important Quote

"You never really understand a person until you consider things from his point of view... until you climb into his skin and walk around in it." — Atticus

This is the novel's central moral lesson — empathy as the foundation of justice and human decency.
