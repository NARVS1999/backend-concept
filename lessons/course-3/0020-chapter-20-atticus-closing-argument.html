Part Three: The Trial — Lesson 9 of 10

Chapter 20 — Atticus's Closing Argument

The case for justice

Focus Atticus delivers his closing argument: the case is simple, the evidence is clear, but the jury must choose justice over prejudice.

Key Details

  • Atticus's closing argument: "This case is as simple as black and white"
  • Only two witnesses testify against Tom — the Ewells
  • "The defendant is not guilty, but somebody in this courtroom is"
  • The real crime: Maycomb's prejudice and Mayella's betrayal
  • "All men are created equal" — but some think they're "more equal"
  • "In the name of God, do your duty"
  • Dolphus Raymond's secret: his bottle contains Coca-Cola

Study Task

Analysis Exercise

Analyze Atticus's "equal before the law" argument. What does he mean that all men are equal "in the courtroom" but not in society?

Important Quote

"In the name of God, do your duty" — Atticus

Atticus appeals to the jury's highest moral authority — asking them to rise above prejudice and deliver justice.
